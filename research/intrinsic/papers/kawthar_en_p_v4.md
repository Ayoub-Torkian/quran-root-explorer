## 5.29 A robustness and generalisation pass — and two corrections

The findings to this point were measured with one family of instruments (verse-level co-occurrence on the rasm) and on one sūra. This section stress-tests them with a *methodologically independent* encoder, generalises the lexical results to the neighbouring compact sūras, and builds two new ranking instruments — closing seven of the open problems listed in §10. The discipline is the same throughout: corpus-internal, null-anchored, and reported whether the result confirms, extends, or **demotes** a prior claim. Two earlier claims in this section are corrected downward here; the core survives and sharpens. Every external analogy (an SVD "encoder", a percolation null) enters only as a null-supplier or cross-check, never as evidence.

### 5.29.1 The headline attraction bond is a single-verse artifact — yet the relational web survives an independent encoder

The most specific lexical claim of §5.23 — that *batr*–*shaniʾ* (بتر–شنء) is "the one strong, statistically notable bond" — does not survive a robustness audit ({{F19}}). The two roots **co-occur in exactly one verse (108:3 itself)**, so the tie is the datum, not a corpus-discovered regularity. Under PPMI, which over-rewards rare pairs, the bond sits at the 99.8th percentile of all 78,111 co-occurring pairs; but under a **count-sensitive** measure (a t-score) it falls to the **79th** — ordinary — and a split-half resampling finds it present only when verse 108:3 happens to be sampled (≈ 48% of halves). Decisively, *naḥr* and *batr* occur once each, so in an **independent SVD word-embedding of the corpus** (a learned low-rank encoder, methodologically independent of the raw PPMI graph) they **have no vector at all**: the bond cannot even be represented, let alone reproduced. [MEASURED on the ROOT substrate; the demotion is itself measured.]

The same independent encoder delivers the converse, and more important, positive result: for **attested** roots the relational structure *reproduces*. The embedding's nearest neighbour of a root lies within that root's top-ten PPMI associates for 33% of frequent roots — about **14× the 2.4% chance rate** — and the neighbourhoods are semantically coherent (*ṣalāt* → سهو, زكو, قوم: the prayer field; *shaniʾ* → قلد, عون, صيد: the al-Māʾida 5:1–2 pilgrimage-law field). The relational web of §5.22–5.23 is therefore **not a co-occurrence residue** — it is recovered by an independent instrument — while the *hapax-specific* bonds are low-count artifacts. This resolves the open robustness question of §10 and the PPMI-inflation caveat: the network's *community/attraction* structure is real; its *rarest individual edges* are not. *Net correction:* §5.23's "specific bond" is downgraded to a single-verse co-occurrence, and the true distributional home of *shaniʾ* is the al-Māʾida legal vocabulary — which, incidentally, is a sounder basis for the §5.24 al-Māʾida link than the bond ever was. [Confidence 90%; flips only if a shrinkage-corrected association still ranks the bond near the top *and* a principled hapax representation reproduces it.]

### 5.29.2 Word-by-word "fittingness" is real — but a general short-sūra property, and the regulative reading is unsupported

The interpretive chapters (§6) were, by our own admission, coherence-compatible rather than design-unique. They can now be given the null they lacked ({{F22}}). Operationalising "fittingness" as a **swap test** — does a sūra's actual content roots cohere (mean pairwise association) more than count-matched random substitutes? — al-Kawthar scores **z = 5.38, the 100th percentile of its own null**: its specific word choices do cohere far above chance. [MEASURED.] But the property is **not unique to it**: among short sūras al-Quraysh (z ≈ 9.9), al-Masad (9.0) and al-Falaq (8.6) score higher, and al-Kawthar is fourth. So "the words fit" is a **measured, genre-level design feature of the short sūras**, not an al-Kawthar exceptionalism — the honest promotion of §6 from "untested reading" to "real but general signal." The narrower **regulative/easing-voice** reading (§5.25, §6.6) fares worse: easing-and-restraint verbs do **not** concentrate in Prophet-office sūras above a matched baseline (the rate is in fact lower; permutation p = 0.99). [MEASURED, though on a coarse address proxy.] That reading stays interpretive, now with a recorded negative. This closes the thematic-null and regulative-null items of §10.

### 5.29.3 Dense singularity is particular, not a length effect — and al-Ikhlāṣ shares the signature

Is al-Kawthar's dense lexical singularity merely what any very short sūra shows? No ({{F20}}, {{T15}}). Of the four compact early sūras, **al-Kawthar (hapax density 0.67/verse, 98th percentile) and al-Ikhlāṣ (0.50, 97th)** carry the signature, while **al-ʿAṣr and al-Naṣr contain no hapax at all** (0th percentile) despite equal brevity. Singularity therefore tracks **the introduction of a defining concept**, not length: al-Ikhlāṣ concentrates its two once-only roots in its theological core — *ṣamad* (the Absolute) and *kufʾ* (comparable, in the negation "none is comparable to Him") — exactly as al-Kawthar concentrates *naḥr*/*kawthar* in its own. The internal-recoverability method thus **generalises selectively**: it applies where a sūra coins a singular term, and there is nothing to recover where it does not. This resolves the cross-text item of §10 and reframes the earlier result as one instance of a small, principled class. [MEASURED on the ROOT substrate.]

### 5.29.4 Singularity is not openness: an explicit (human-construct) openness gradient

The §6.7 argument that *kawthar* is referentially **open** can now be given a reproducible — if explicitly constructed — ranking ({{F21}}). An openness score built from three internal proxies (specificity = mean idf; singularity = hapax density; and *low* self-interpretation support = few corpus neighbours to fix meaning) ranks **al-Kawthar 1st of 114** in internal under-determination. The instructive contrast is al-Ikhlāṣ: equally singular, it ranks only **57th** — *singular but anchored*, because *ṣamad* is theologically fixed and its companions (*aḥad*, *Allāh*) are among the most connected roots in the corpus. **Singularity is therefore not openness**: rare vocabulary can be tightly determined (al-Ikhlāṣ) or radically open (al-Kawthar). The honest bound is twofold: the score correlates with specificity *by construction*, so its informative content is the **residual** — that al-Ikhlāṣ sits well below the trend its rarity would predict; and, per the *taʾwīl*-humble limit (§6.7), this instrument **localises and ranks** openness — it never adjudicates the *muḥkam*/*mutashābih* categories. [MEASURED as a **[HUMAN CONSTRUCT]** ranking; flips if a support measure that is not idf-correlated re-orders the top.]

### 5.29.5 "The long sūra elaborates the short" is not a systematic feature

Finally, the suggestion in §5.24 that a long sūra (al-Māʾida) "elaborates" al-Kawthar is **not generalisable** ({{F23}}). Running a length-normalised, rarity-weighted elaboration score over all eleven short sūras, **none** has a long sūra as its top elaborator: the mean size of the top elaborators is 14 roots against a random-sūra mean of 157 (permutation p = 1.0). After controlling for length, the best per-root co-thematic match for a short sūra is **another short sūra**. al-Kawthar's own top elaborator is **al-Ḍuḥā (93)** — both early sūras of consolation-and-gift addressed to the Prophet, a far more coherent pairing than al-Māʾida. This **corrects §5.24**: the al-Māʾida link is real only as the *shaniʾ* lexical thread (§5.29.1), not as an "elaboration", and long→short elaboration is not a property of the muṣḥaf. [MEASURED; the measure captures co-thematic density, not demonstrated intent — that caveat is unchanged.]

### 5.29.6 A concept-coining typology: al-Kawthar and al-Ikhlāṣ are the only short sūras with two distinct coinages

The cross-text result (§5.29.3) sharpens into a measured typology ({{F24}}). Classifying every short sūra (≤ 6 verses) by its hapax, and marking a hapax **distinct** when its rare context is shared with no other once-only root (the §5.27.2 criterion), the short sūras separate into four clean classes: **doubly concept-coining** — *two* distinct hapax — which is **only al-Kawthar (*naḥr*, *abtar*) and al-Ikhlāṣ (*ṣamad*, *kufʾ*)**; **singly-coining** (al-Falaq *nafth*; al-Fīl *fīl*); **object-naming**, whose hapax share contexts and name concrete things (al-Quraysh: *shitāʾ* "winter", *ṣayf* "summer"; al-Masad: *masad* "palm-fibre rope"); and **no-coinage** (al-ʿAṣr, al-Naṣr, al-Kāfirūn, al-Nās, al-Qadr). The positive finding is the **two-member doubly-coining class**: al-Kawthar and al-Ikhlāṣ are the only short sūras that each introduce *two* referentially-distinct terms, and in both they are the sūra's theological pivots — the gift/severance pair, and the Absolute/incomparable pair. [MEASURED on the ROOT substrate.] *Honest bound:* "distinct" measures *unshared rare context*, not abstractness as such — al-Fīl's *fīl* (elephant) is concrete yet distinct — so the abstract-concept gloss of the class is **[INFERRED]**; what is measured is the unique double-distinctness of 108 and 112. [Confidence 85%; flips if a finer concreteness measure dissolves the class.]
