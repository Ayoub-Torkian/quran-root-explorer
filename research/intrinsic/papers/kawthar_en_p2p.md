## 5.20.3 The call (*daʿwa*): the absolute-good word, in the finest manner

The thread the questioner draws is real and tightly woven by a single root, *d-ʿ-w* ("to call, invite"; 212×). Three verses lock together:

- **What is the absolute-good word?** "Who has a **better word** (*aḥsanu qawlan*) than one who **calls to God** (*daʿā ilā Allāh*) and acts righteously…?" (41:33) — the finest speech *is* the call to God [TEXT].
- **What is the Prophet's way?** "Say: this is **my way** — I **call to God** (*adʿū ilā Allāh*) with insight, I and whoever follows me" (12:108) — the prophetic vocation simply *is* this calling [TEXT].
- **In what manner is the call made?** "**Call to the way of your Lord** (*udʿu ilā sabīli rabbika*) with wisdom and goodly admonition, and **argue with them in the way that is best** (*bi-llatī hiya aḥsan*)" (16:125) [TEXT].

Set these beside the "repel" verses (§5.20) and a single ethic emerges, because **they share both the root *d-ʿ-w* and the formula *bi-llatī hiya aḥsan***: the absolute-good response to people — whether one is *calling* them (16:125) or *repelling* their ill conduct (23:96; 41:34) — is the **finest, good-as-such** word and deed, the call to God conducted in the best manner. And the call is **to the way of *your Lord*** (*rabbika*, 16:125; cf. "call upon *your Lord*," 7:55) — the same nurturing *rabb* to whom verse 108:2 turns in prayer (§5.18). The *daʿwa* is, moreover, **bidirectional and grace-bound**: God Himself "**calls** to the abode of peace" (10:25) and "**calls** you that He may forgive you" (14:10), and "I answer the **call** of the caller when he calls Me" (2:186). The servant calls upon the Lord; the Lord calls to peace and pardon; the Prophet calls others to the Lord in the finest manner — one root, one current.

Folded into al-Kawthar: the Prophet, *given* the abundance and told to turn to his Lord (verses 1–2), is by vocation the *dāʿī ilā Allāh* (12:108), whose speech is the *aḥsanu qawlan* (41:33). His way of meeting opposition is therefore the *daʿwa* "in the way that is best" (16:125) — the same *bi-llatī hiya aḥsan* that can turn an enemy into "a warm friend" (41:34). The good word, not the elimination of the speaker, is the instrument.

**Critical caveats (kept, per §7).** Two honesty checks. First, the *level* problem again: Sūra 108 itself contains **neither** *d-ʿ-w* nor *aḥsan*; the *daʿwa*-ethic is the wider current the surah *participates in*, established from 12:108 / 16:125 / 41:33 — it is **context, not 108-internal** lexicon. We mark it as such. Second — and this is a genuine **tension within the surah**, which the surrounding sections risk smoothing over — verse 108:3 is **not** an instance of "repel with the better / turn the enemy into a friend." It is a **verdict**: the hater *is* the cut-off one. The gentle *dafʿ*/*daʿwa* ethic (redeem the actor, 41:34) and the surah's own closing **condemnation** (the actor is *abtar*) sit in real tension; 108:3 is, in tone, nearer the naming-and-sealing of Abu Lahab (111) than the enemy-to-friend of 41:34. The honest reading holds both without collapsing one into the other: the believer's *assigned action* is the good word and deed (the *daʿwa* in the finest manner), while the hater's *fate*, left to his own hatred, is severance. The surah pairs an ethic of the best response **with** a verdict of self-incurred loss; it is not a pure manual of conciliation. **[INFERRED, moderate]** for the unifying *daʿwa* ethic; **[flagged tension]** between that ethic and 108:3's verdict — recorded, not resolved.
