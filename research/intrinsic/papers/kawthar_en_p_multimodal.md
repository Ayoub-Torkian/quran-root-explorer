## 5.28 A multimodal test: is the distinctiveness one channel or several?

The singularity established in §5.27 is *lexical*. A natural and falsifiable question follows: does al-Kawthar's distinctiveness hold up in **other, independent channels** — letters, sound, morphology, information content — or is it one phenomenon seen once? We tested four candidate channels, each with a **length-matched null** (random spans of al-Kawthar's exact 43-letter length, so shortness cannot manufacture the effect) and an **independence check** (correlation with the lexical channel). The discipline matters: two candidates passed, two failed, and reporting both is the point.

| channel | metric | al-Kawthar percentile (length-matched) | independent of lexical? | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Lexical | rare-root (hapax) density / verse | 97th | — (the reference) | **VALIDATED** (§5.27.2) |
| Graphemic | rare letter-bigram density | **98th** | yes (r = 0.38) | **VALIDATED — new independent axis** |

| Information | compressibility (incompressible = dense) | length-confounded | — | **REJECTED** — intrinsic short-text effect |

Two findings stand. First, the **graphemic channel is a genuinely independent axis**: al-Kawthar packs *rare letter-pairs* (bigrams occurring in ≤2 sūras) at the 98th percentile of equal-length random spans, and this barely correlates with its rare-*word* density (r = 0.38) — so it is not lexical singularity re-expressed but a separate fact about how its (small) alphabet is combined into unusual pairs. Second, **two plausible channels fail their length-matched null** — the surah's low sound-entropy and high incompressibility are *artifacts of its shortness*, not distinctions — and we record them as rejected rather than quietly drop them.

The validated picture is therefore precise and bounded: **al-Kawthar's distinctiveness is two-dimensional and convergent** — it sits at the *joint* extreme of two independent, length-validated channels, rare **words** and rare **letter-pairs** ({{F18}}). It is not the top of either axis alone (al-Quraysh 106 has higher rare-root density; al-Sharḥ 94 higher rare-bigrams), but it is the **only** sūra extreme on *both* at once. This is the honest scope of the claim: a real, replicated, multi-channel singularity — not a distinction on every lens (it is ordinary on binding, transport, phonological entropy, and information density), but a convergent one on the two channels that survive a proper null.
