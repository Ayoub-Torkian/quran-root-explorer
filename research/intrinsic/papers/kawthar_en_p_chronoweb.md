**Part E — Generalisation: the surah's pair is one edge of a corpus-wide chronology web.**

## 5.31 From the pair to the chronology web

The al-Ḍuḥā→al-Kawthar promise→fulfilment link of §5.30 is not an isolated coincidence; it is one **edge** of a larger object. Applying the same method corpus-wide — pairing forms, tracking a concept's development through all its occurrences, and anchoring to datable references — yields not a single timeline but a **partial-order graph**: several parallel developmental threads braided through shared time-windows ({{F27}}). al-Kawthar's pair is the seed edge from which the construction was generalised.

**What internal evidence recovers.** A learner trained only on the rasm separates Meccan from Medinan sūras at AUC 0.86, and internal style + referent features recover the traditional revelation order at a pairwise concordance of 0.74 (shuffle-null ≈ 0). [MEASURED on Book6, rasm-WORD substrate.] The referent-groups date themselves by when they enter the discourse: the Meccan adversaries (المکذّبون, المشرکون) are early; the Medinan community referents (المنافقون 97% late, أهل الکتاب 96% late, النصاری appearing only late) are late. [MEASURED.] Three legal concepts trace internally-verified **deontic escalations** — khamr (provision سکراً → س.219 إثم → 4:43 لا تقربوا → 5:90 اجتنبوا), qitāl (کفّوا → أُذن → کُتب → فاقتلوا), and ribā (neutral → حرّم) — and the rhetorical register shifts monotonically from pure warning to glad-tidings (nadhīr-share 100% → 75% → 53% across early/mid/late). [MEASURED.]

**What is corroboration, not evidence.** Datable events (the qibla change, Badr, Uhud, the Aḥzāb, Ḥudaybiyya, the conquest, Tabūk, the Najrān/Mubāhala challenge 3:61, and the household episodes 33:37/53, 24:11, 66:1) supply the **time coordinates** on the graph. Each was reconciled internally — the cited āyahs were verified to carry the event's own Qurʾanic vocabulary (qibla → شطر/المسجد الحرام; Badr → بدر, named at 3:123; Aḥzāb → جند/إذ جاءتکم جنود; Mubāhala → نبتهل) — 12 of 12 anchors reconciling. But the dates themselves are *asbāb al-nuzūl* / sīra: **external transmission of varying authenticity, corroborative at most, never asserted as fact.** [REPORT.]

**The structure is a partial order, not a line.** Forcing a linear rank onto the Medinan material fails — internal features cannot recover within-Medinan order (per-āyah features null against the revelation column), because much of it is genuinely **contemporaneous, not sequenced**: at ~2 AH, Badr, the qibla change, the khamr "sin & benefit" ruling, and the emergence of the hypocrite and People-of-Book referents co-occur as a *class*, not a sequence. A single sūra can therefore be **multi-temporal**: Āl ʿImrān holds a 3 AH chunk (Uhud, *القرح*) and a ~9-10 AH chunk (the Najrān/Mubāhala argument) under one revelation number — direct evidence that dating must operate on chunks, not whole sūras. Across the assembled graph, 23–60% of node-pairs are incomparable (the figure depends on how much datable anchoring is admitted), and the directed "spine" is far shorter than the node set. [INFERRED from the measured nulls and the anchor reconciliation; the reconstruction is a HUMAN CONSTRUCT re-indexing — explorable, never presented as divine structure.]

**The multi-temporal pattern is corpus-wide — and tradition independently attests it.** Beyond Āl ʿImrān, the traditional revelation lists (Ibn ʿAbbās / al-Zanjānī) flag embedded verses of the opposite era in roughly thirty sūras [REPORT]. Tested against those lists, the internal āyah-level detector — built only on the rasm and blind to them — flags the *same sūras* as carrying Medinan material, and its flag *includes* the traditional verses with high recall (al-Aʿrāf 163-170 all 8, al-Shūrā 23-27 all 4, al-Anʿām 7 of 8, al-Aḥqāf 3 of 3). But it **over-flags**: precision is low (it marks roughly half of a long sūra), so the agreement is reliable at the *sūra* level — *this sūra is temporally mixed* — and not at the precise verse. [MEASURED: recall high on ~7 sūras, precision <0.2 throughout; the detector localises the mixed sūra, it does not pin the exact verses.] Even so, internal evidence and traditional report independently agree on *which* sūras are multi-temporal — confirming the chunk, not the sūra, is the unit of dating, while precise verse-dating is left to closer reading.

**al-Kawthar's place in the web.** The sūra sits on the *ritual + promise* thread, beside al-Ḍuḥā; its perfect *aʿṭaynāka* closes the future *yuʿṭīka* of 93:5 as the gift-edge of the promise-ledger (§5.30). The division of labour the web makes explicit is the same one this whole study has observed in miniature: computation **localises and ranks** candidates for human reading — it placed al-Ḍuḥā as al-Kawthar's nearest sūra — while the grammar, the concordance, and (only as corroboration) the historical record supply the meaning. The chronology is thus offered not as a settled sequence but as a navigable, falsifiable map of the territory (Figure 27).
