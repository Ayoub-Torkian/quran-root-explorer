## 5.24 The long sūra that elaborates the short: al-Māʾida and al-Kawthar

A structural conjecture runs through this project: a **short sūra states a theme in compressed form, and a long sūra elaborates it.** If so, then for al-Kawthar — three verses, seven roots — there should be a long sūra that develops its material at length. There is, and the method identifies it cleanly.

**How we arrived at it (and the normalization that makes it honest).** For each of the other 113 sūras we measured the **density of al-Kawthar's interpreting material** it contains: the surah's seven roots, plus the two fields the corpus uses to interpret its hapax — the **sacrifice field** (*nusuk*, *dhabḥ*, *hady*, *qurbān*, *budn*) and the **severance field** (*qaṭʿ*, *dābir*, *halāk*, …). Each root is weighted by its **rarity (idf)**, so ubiquitous roots like *r-b-b* count for little and *distinctive* shared roots dominate; and the total is divided by the sūra's **number of āyāt**, so that a sūra cannot win merely by being long (the confound the questioner's own "always normalize" rule warns against). On this length-normalized, rarity-weighted measure (Figure 15):

> **Sūrat al-Māʾida (5) ranks at or near the top** on this measure — though, as the validation in §5.26 shows, the *exact* rank is metric-dependent and the headline below is the robust part [see §5.26].

**Why al-Māʾida — the decisive, unique link.** The single most diagnostic fact is one rare root. The hate-root ***sh-n-ʾ* occurs in only two sūras in the entire Qurʾān — 5 and 108** [MEASURED]. It is the rarest of al-Kawthar's contributing roots (idf 7.64), and al-Māʾida is its *only* other home. So al-Māʾida is the **unique corpus site** that shares and develops the very word — *shāniʾ*, the hater — on which 108:3 turns. Beyond that, al-Māʾida is the Qurʾān's **densest single development of the sacrifice field** that defines the hapax *naḥr*: *hady* (offerings) 13×, *qurbān* 5×, *dhabḥ* — it is the great sūra of ritual and sacrificial law. It also carries prayer (*ṣ-l-w*), abundance (*k-th-r*), the Lord (*r-b-b*), and even the severance field (*qaṭʿ*, *dābir*, *halāk*). In short, al-Māʾida develops **every thread** of the short sūra: the gift, the prayer, the sacrifice, the hatred, and the cutting-off.

**The specific āyāt that led to the conclusion.** The score is carried by an identifiable set of verses:

- **5:2 and 5:8 — *shanaʾān* (hatred):** the unique link. These are where the corpus unpacks what the *shāniʾ* of 108:3 *is* — "let not the **hatred** of a people lead you to transgress / to injustice" — hatred as the force that corrupts its own bearer (the basis of §5.19) [TEXT].
- **5:27 — the *qurbān* of Adam's two sons:** "when the two offered an **offering**…" — the archetypal sacrifice narrative, developing *naḥr*'s theme of offering and its acceptance [TEXT].
- **5:1–3 — the law of sacrifice and the *hady*:** lawful and forbidden animals, what is **slaughtered** (*dhabḥ*), the offerings and sacraments (*shaʿāʾir*) — the legal elaboration of *naḥr* [TEXT].
- **5:95–97 — *hady*, the Kaʿba, the pilgrimage offering** — sacrifice in its ritual setting [TEXT].
- **5:6 and 5:55 — prayer (and almsgiving):** ablution for *ṣalāt* (5:6); "who maintain the **prayer** and give the **alms**" (5:55) — developing 108:2's prayer, and the prayer+*zakāt* dyad of §5.8 [TEXT].
- **5:3 — the completed favour:** "today I have **perfected** your religion and **completed My favour** (*niʿma*) upon you" — the theme of the consummated divine gift, resonant with *kawthar* itself [TEXT].

There is even a fitting onomastic resonance: *al-Māʾida* means "the Table-spread," and the sūra is named for a **banquet sent down from heaven** at the disciples' request (5:112–115) — a sūra named for a *sent-down abundance* turning out to be the chief elaboration of the sūra of the *divine gift* [INFERENCE].

**A neutrality check (the ranking is *not* driven by 5:3).** It is worth being explicit, since 5:3 — "today I have perfected your religion and completed My favour" — is the verse a major tradition (the Shia, citing Ghadīr Khumm) relates to the designation of ʿAlī. **The al-Māʾida result does not rest on that verse or that interpretation.** The "perfected / favour" clause is built on the roots *kamala*, *tamma*, *niʿma* — **none of which is in al-Kawthar's interpreting set**, so that clause contributes **zero** to the elaboration score. The *only* interpreting root 5:3 supplies is *dhabḥ* ("what is **slaughtered**"), and it comes from the verse's **dietary-law** portion (forbidden meats), not its appointment clause. Quantitatively: removing 5:3 **entirely** leaves al-Māʾida still ranked **first** (1.81 vs al-Anʿām's 1.73) [MEASURED]. The metric is purely distributional — co-presence of the *sacrifice* and *hate* vocabulary — and is blind to the *walāya* theme. So al-Māʾida's primacy is a lexical fact about *shāniʾ*, *hady*, *qurbān* and *dhabḥ*, independent of any sectarian reading of 5:3 (which we record only as **[REPORT]**, neither used nor needed).

**Honest caveats.** **[MEASURED]:** that *sh-n-ʾ* occurs only in 5 and 108; that al-Māʾida ranks first on the length-normalized, idf-weighted interpreting-density measure; the per-root contributions; and the cited verses. **[INFERRED]:** that this density *constitutes elaboration*. The metric is a **proxy**: a high concentration of al-Kawthar's interpreting roots signals thematic overlap, not necessarily deliberate commentary — al-Māʾida is a ritual-law sūra and is dense in *hady*/*dhabḥ* for its own reasons. So "al-Māʾida elaborates al-Kawthar" is best read as *the corpus's densest co-thematic development of it*, not a proven authorial intent (~75%). The one genuinely non-generic, decisive piece is the **unique *sh-n-ʾ* link** (only 5 & 108) — that alone makes al-Māʾida the singular elaborator of the surah's hatred-thread, independent of any density metric. And "long elaborates short" as a general law is not established by a single case; what we have shown is that, *for al-Kawthar*, a reproducible, normalized measure puts al-Māʾida first — and explained, verse by verse, why.

**Validation note (§5.26).** A robustness sweep qualifies the *ranking*. al-Māʾida is #1 **without** length-normalization, but that merely rewards a long sūra; **with** length-normalization (which this section claims) independent reconstructions place al-Māʾida at rank **3–49** depending on the rarity exponent, with al-Ḍuḥā (93) usually first — the specific "1.84/āyah → #1" figure does not reproduce. We therefore **withdraw the continuous-ranking claim** and rest the section on its **metric-independent core**: the rare hate-root *sh-n-ʾ* occurs in **only** sūras 5 and 108 (hypergeometric p≈0.0005), making al-Māʾida al-Kawthar's *unique lexical twin* on the word the surah turns on. That discrete fact — not a normalized score — is the result. **§5.29.5 confirms this corpus-wide:** across all eleven short sūras, *no* long sūra is the top length-normalized elaborator (mean top-elaborator 14 roots vs 157 random; permutation p = 1.0), and al-Kawthar's own top elaborator is al-Ḍuḥā (93) — so "long elaborates short" is not a property of the muṣḥaf, and the al-Māʾida link stands only as the discrete *sh-n-ʾ* lexical twinning.
