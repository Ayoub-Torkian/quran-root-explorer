## 5.17.1 Why *wa-nḥar* is indispensable: the act, the intention, and the giving that attains *birr*

A fair objection to §5.4 and §5.17 is that "sacrifice as the total offering" does not yet say *why naḥr*, specifically, must stand beside prayer — it can feel decorative. The corpus answers on three internal levels, the first two of them stated outright and the third joined by a shared root.

**First — the act is a vehicle; the intention is what ascends.** The Qur'an says of sacrifice, in the plainest terms, that the ritual itself reaches nothing — only the inner state does: "**It is not their flesh, nor their blood, that reaches (*yanāl*) God; rather, it is the piety (*taqwā*) from you that reaches (*yanāl*) Him**" (22:37) [TEXT]. So *naḥr* is "just an act," and its whole worth is the **taqwā / intention** behind it — that is the anchor, and that is what *ascends*. This is why verse 2 can command an outward rite without reducing worship to ritual: the rite is the body, *taqwā* is the soul that "reaches" God.

**Second — the act is a symbol of belonging.** The same passage frames the sacrificial animals as "**among the symbols (*shaʿāʾir*) of God**" (22:36) [TEXT]. *Naḥr* is therefore not a transaction but a **token of devoted belonging** — a public, embodied sign that one stands with God, of a piece with *ṣalli li-rabbika* ("turn to *your Lord*").

**Third — *naḥr* is the paradigm of "giving from what you love," the path to *birr*.** Here a lexical bridge does real work. The verb that 22:37 uses for what "reaches" God — root *n-y-l* — is the very verb of 3:92: "**You will never *attain* (*tanālū*) *birr* (righteous piety) until you spend from what you *love* (*tuḥibbūn*)**" [TEXT]. The two verses lock together: *taqwā* is what *reaches* God (22:37), and *birr* is what *you attain*, by **giving up what you cherish** (3:92). *Naḥr* — the slaughter and giving-away of a valued, beloved animal — is the concrete instance of exactly that "spending from what you love." And 2:177 both **roots and unfolds** *birr*: it begins by grounding it in **faith** — "**but *birr* is [in] whoever believes in God, the Last Day, the angels, the Book, and the prophets**" — and only then lists its acts: "**gives wealth, out of love for it**, to kinsfolk, orphans and the needy… **establishes prayer** and **gives the alms**… and keeps covenant… and is patient" [TEXT]. Two things follow. First, *birr* is **faith made manifest in deed** — its taproot is belief, its fruit is giving-and-prayer; so the *taqwā* that "reaches God" in 22:37 is that same inner faith, and *naḥr* is its embodiment. Second, 2:177 bundles **costly giving with ṣalāt** in one definition of *birr* — the very couplet of 108:2. So "pray and sacrifice" is, in the corpus's own terms, *the prayer-and-costly-giving, springing from faith, that constitutes birr.*

**The social dimension follows directly.** Because the sacrifice is not consumed alone but shared — "eat thereof **and feed the destitute and the needy**" (22:28; 22:36) — *naḥr* is also an act of **distribution**: giving from one's valued wealth to the poor. Read socially, verse 2 prescribes a community in which received abundance is *turned to God and shared out*, not consumed or stored — the practical shape of communal *birr* and well-being. This is where the surah's directive touches social life: the gift of *kawthar* is meant to **circulate**.

**A complementary note (held as inference).** This also coheres with the reading of §5.2: since the gift is *abundance* and the root *k-th-r* warns against *amassing* it (*takāthur*), *naḥr* — giving from the abundance — runs counter to hoarding. We keep that as a *complementary* observation rather than the main pillar, because the Qur'an never pairs *naḥr* and *takāthur* in one verse (the antithesis is constructed), whereas the *taqwā*-reaches (22:37), the *shaʿāʾir* (22:36), and the *n-y-l* tie to *birr*-by-giving-what-you-love (3:92; 2:177) are **stated or lexical**. So *wa-nḥar* is indispensable on the strongest internal grounds: it is the embodied, intention-anchored, wealth-distributing half of the response — the giving-from-what-you-love by which *birr* is attained, paired with the *ṣalāt* by which one turns to the Giver. [TEXT: 22:36; 22:37; 22:28; 3:92; 2:177 — with the *n-y-l* link between 22:37 and 3:92 measured. INFERRED: that *naḥr* is *the paradigm case* of 3:92's "spend what you love" (3:92 and 2:177 speak of *infāq* in general), and the social-distribution and anti-*takāthur* readings.]
