## 5.30 The grammar of the gift: tense, mood, person, and the pronoun of separation

Everything to this point — the lexical core, the web, the rarity stratum, the robustness pass — worked on the **ROOT** substrate: content roots with grammar stripped away. Yet al-Kawthar's meaning lives substantially in the grammar of its ten words — the function-words and verb-forms the root reduction discards. Read at the **rasm-WORD layer** (consonantal grammar, not the later vowel-points), the surah yields readings no distributional instrument could reach. Each is **[TEXT]** — a property of the text itself, attested from the Qurʾān's own usage ({{F25}}).

**The tense-antithesis with al-Ḍuḥā: promise → fulfilment.** The gift-verb *ʿ-ṭ-w* occurs in only twelve places in the whole Qurʾān, and two of them are the paired sūras of consolation to the Prophet. al-Ḍuḥā 93:5 — *wa-la-sawfa **yuʿṭīka** rabbuk* — is an emphatic **future**: "your Lord *shall* give you, and you will be well-pleased." al-Kawthar 108:1 — *innā **aʿṭaynāka*** — is a **perfect**: "We *have* given you." The same divine gift, to the same recipient, is *promised* in al-Ḍuḥā and *declared accomplished* in al-Kawthar. al-Kawthar is the perfect-tense **fulfilment** of al-Ḍuḥā's future-tense **pledge**. This is the textual reason beneath the computational pairing of §5.29.5: the instrument could only *localise* al-Ḍuḥā as al-Kawthar's nearest sūra; the grammar shows *why* the two belong together. [TEXT; the twelve *ʿ-ṭ-w* sites are corpus-confirmed, the tense read off the rasm.]

**Two names of the Giver, one per move.** Verse 1 gives in the **plural of majesty** — *innā aʿṭaynā*, "**We** gave," the register of sovereign power. Verse 2 turns to *li-**rabbika***, "for **your Lord**" — singular, intimate, the possessive *-ka* binding the Lord to this one servant. The Giver names Himself by *power* in the act of giving and by *intimate Lordship* in calling for the response. The choice of the majestic plural was the measured point of §5.10; what is new here is the **contrast** it strikes, inside three words, against the singular *rabbika*. [TEXT.]

**The fāʾ of consequence.** The command opens with a *fāʾ*: *fa-ṣalli*, "**so** pray." It is the *fāʾ al-sababiyya / al-tartīb*, the particle of consequence: worship follows *from* the gift already given (the perfect of verse 1), not as a price for a gift still to come. The whole gift-and-response economy of §5.8 turns, grammatically, on a single letter — the response is gratitude for a *completed* act, not a payment toward a pending one. [TEXT.]

**The pronoun of separation seals the inversion.** Verse 3 — *inna shāniʾaka **huwa** l-abtar* — sets the *ḍamīr al-faṣl* (the "pronoun of separation") *huwa* between subject and predicate, and makes the predicate definite, *al-abtar* ("**the** cut-off one"). Both devices do one thing: **restriction**. The verse does not say merely "your hater is cut off"; it says "your hater — *he* [and he alone] is *the* cut-off one." The *kawthar*↔*abtar* inversion the root-level reading found (§5.3) is, at the level of grammar, **sealed exclusively onto the hater**: the taunt is not just returned but categorically and solely reassigned. The *ḍamīr al-faṣl* carries exactly this restrictive force across the Qurʾān — e.g. *ulāʾika **hum** al-mufliḥūn*, "those — *they* [alone] are the ones who prosper." [TEXT.]

**The mood ladder is the agency ladder.** Read by mood, the three verses climb a determinate ladder: **perfect** (verse 1 — God acts, completed), **imperative** (verse 2 — the Prophet is commanded, action enjoined), **equational / verbless** (verse 3 — a settled state, a verdict pronounced). The agency progression the root-structure noted (§5.5) is carried *precisely* by grammatical mood: the doer of verse 1 is God in the accomplished past, of verse 2 the Prophet under present command, and of verse 3 no new doer at all — only a standing judgment. [TEXT.]

**The promise–fulfilment ledger (al-Kawthar is one of three sealed worldly gifts).** The tense-antithesis is one entry in a small, *complete* ledger ({{F26}}). Reading every prospective construction addressed to the Prophet — future *sa-/sawfa*, *ʿasā*, and the emphatic *nūn* — the Qurʾan makes God **six** forward-looking promises to him. **Three concern this world and are declared *kept* in the perfect**: the **gift** — *yuʿṭīka* (93:5) → *aʿṭaynāka l-kawthar* (108:1); **protection** — *sa-yakfīkahum* (2:137) → *kafaynāka l-mustahziʾīn* (15:95); and the **qibla** — *la-nuwalliyannaka qiblatan tarḍāhā* (2:144), enacted in the very same verse (*fa-walli wajhaka*). **Three stay grammatically *open*, and by their nature must**: the eschatological **praised station** (*ʿasā an yabʿathaka… maqāman maḥmūdan*, 17:79 — granted only at the Resurrection) and the two **revelation** promises (*sanuqriʾuka* 87:6, *sanulqī ʿalayka qawlan* 73:5 — the giving of the Qurʾan is ongoing, never a sealed past act). The discharged/open split is therefore **principled**, not arbitrary: worldly favours are sealed in the perfect; revelation and the eschaton remain open. al-Kawthar sits among the three sealed worldly gifts — and is the **gift**. (A narrower thread: the *riḍā* terminus — *fa-tarḍā* / *tarḍāhā* — marks exactly **two** of the six, the gift (93:5) and the qibla (2:144): the two promises of the Prophet's *contentment*. It is *not* the signature of the discharged class — protection (2:137) is discharged without it.) **The gift is a *chronological* fulfilment, not only a grammatical one.** On the revelation-order axis (a legitimate divine-alt arrangement), the gift pair is sequenced exactly as the grammar implies: al-Ḍuḥā was revealed **11th**, al-Kawthar **15th** — the future pledge first, the perfect *aʿṭaynāka* afterward. So al-Kawthar is the *later* discharge of an *earlier* promise in real revealed time, not merely in tense. This is **not** a general law — the protection pair runs the other way (the perfect 15:95 was revealed 54th, before the future 2:137 at 87th) — which itself is telling: **only for the gift do grammatical and chronological fulfilment coincide.** [MEASURED on Book6 revelation-order at sūra granularity; the pairing is content-judged.]

**A divine-deixis law: God *acts* as “We”, but is *worshipped* as “the Lord”.** The register-switch noted above (We give → pray to *your Lord*) is a corpus-wide rule. Worship-verbs (*ʿabada, sajada, ṣallā, sabbaḥa, ḥamida*) take **“Allah / your Lord”** as their object in ≈160 verses and the majestic **“We/Us” in none**; even where the acting “We” shares a verse with a worship-command, the object is invariably “Allah” — *innā anzalnā… fa-ʿbudi llāh* (39:2, “We revealed… so worship Allah”). The first-person plural is the voice of divine *action and giving*; the One *worshipped* is named “the Lord / Allah.” **Sharper still:** worship attaches only to the *singular* God — “Me” (1st-sg, e.g. 20:14 *innanī anā llāhu… fa-ʿbudnī*) or “the Lord/Allah” (≈194 verses) — and to the majestic plural “We” in **none** (0 of all worship occurrences). The plurality of “We” is thus a plurality of *power and action*, categorically barred from being the object of worship: *tawḥīd* requires the singular. al-Kawthar states the whole law in three words — *innā aʿṭaynā* (We gave) → *ṣalli li-rabbika* (pray to your Lord). [MEASURED on Book6: worship-root × divine-name co-occurrence corpus-wide; the zero worship-of-“We” confirmed by inspecting the “We”+worship verses.]

**What this adds.** None of these readings is distributional; each is a property of the *text's own grammar* — the layer the root substrate set aside — and each is attested from the Qurʾān's usage. Together they compress the surah's argument to one sentence whose every joint is grammatical: *because* the gift is **already given** (perfect; majestic *We*), **therefore** (*fāʾ*) turn in worship to **your Lord** (intimate *-ka*); **as for** your hater, **he** — exclusively — is **the** one cut off (*ḍamīr al-faṣl* + definite). The computation localised and ranked; the grammar reads. This is the layer where new *understanding* — not new statistics — was still to be found, and it is the natural next instrument for the program (the morphology lever of §10).
