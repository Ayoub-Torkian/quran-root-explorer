## 5.18 The Lord who keeps giving: nurture, the rain on a people, and gratitude-as-growth

Two further word-choices in verse 2 open the gift onto **continuity** — onto a relationship that keeps giving rather than a transaction that closes.

**"To your Lord" (*li-rabbika*), not "to your Creator."** The verse could have said *ṣalli li-khāliqika*, "pray to your Creator." It says *li-rabbika*, "to your **Lord**." The difference is not decorative. *Khalq* (to create, 261×) is the act of **originating** — a thing brought into being. *Rabb* (980×) names the one who **rears, nurtures, sustains, brings up by stages** — and the Qur'an proves this sense from its own mouth: Pharaoh says to Moses, "Did we not **rear** you (*nurabbika*, root *r-b-b*) as a child among us?" (26:18), and the child is taught to pray for parents "as they **reared** me (*rabbayānī*) when I was small" (17:24) [TEXT]. The root is the vocabulary of **upbringing**. To direct worship "to your *Lord*" is therefore to face the One in an **ongoing, developing relationship** — the Sustainer who keeps enabling and building, not merely the Originator who once made. The continuity we traced in *kawthar* (§5.14) is thus written into the very name the surah uses for God: the gift comes from the One whose defining work is to *keep* nurturing.

**The same abundance, poured on a whole people.** What *kawthar* is to the individual Prophet, the Qur'an describes for the righteous **community** — and here the continuity is unmistakable, because the imagery is of *flowing*. "Had the people of the towns believed and been Godwary, We would have **opened** upon them **blessings** (*barakāt*) from the heaven and the earth" (7:96); "had they kept to the path, We would have given them **abundant water** (*māʾan ghadaqā*)" (72:16); seek forgiveness, and "He will send the sky upon you in **pouring rain** (*midrārā*), and aid you with wealth and sons, and gardens, and **streams**" (71:11–12) [TEXT]. *Barakāt* (root *b-r-k*) is a word of abundance, but abundance that *abides and grows*; the verbs are of **opening** and **pouring**; and — fittingly — *ghadaq*, "abundant water," is itself a **hapax** (its only occurrence, 72:16), joining *kawthar*, *naḥr*, and *abtar* in this study's striking gallery of once-used words for the extremes of plenty and loss. The point for us: the gift to the Prophet is one instance of a standing law — right standing draws down a **continuous shower** of blessing, quantity (*much*) and continuity (*flowing*) together, exactly the two axes of §5.14, now visible at the scale of a people.

**Gratitude that grows the self.** How is such giving sustained? By a dynamic the Qur'an states with a precision the user rightly flags. "And when your Lord proclaimed: **'If you are grateful, I will surely increase you* (*la-azīdannakum*)*'**" (14:7) [TEXT]. Note what the verse does **not** say: it does not say "if you thank Me, I will give you *more things*." Its object is **you** — *azīdanna-**kum***, "I will increase **you**." The return on gratitude is not a fresh delivery of goods but an **enlargement of the self** — the grateful person is *grown*, raised in capacity, "increased." This is the *rabb* again, the nurturer, doing what nurturers do: developing the one in their care. And gratitude here is no mere word, for *shukr* in the Qur'an has **action built in**: "**Work** (*iʿmalū*), O family of David, in **gratitude** (*shukran*)" (34:13) — thanks expressed as *labour*, as walking the talk [TEXT]. Which closes the circle with verse 2: the gift of *kawthar* is answered by *shukr* **enacted** — prayer and sacrifice, gratitude as deed, not declaration — and by 14:7 that grateful action does not merely earn a payout; it **enlarges the doer**. The gift, gratefully and actively received, increases the receiver's very capacity to receive and to give: a spiral of nurture, not a closed exchange.

Read into the surah: God *gives* (verse 1, the *rabb*'s bounty); the servant returns *active gratitude* to that same nurturing Lord (verse 2, *ṣalli li-rabbika wa-nḥar* — *shukr* as deed); and by the law of 14:7 such gratitude *increases the servant himself*. The continuity is total: a Lord who keeps rearing, a gift that keeps flowing, a gratitude that keeps growing the one who offers it.

We mark the confidence. **[MEASURED]:** *rabb* (980) vs *khalq* (261), and the *r-b-b* = "rear/bring-up" sense proven at 26:18 and 17:24; the collective-abundance verses 7:96 (*barakāt*), 72:16 (*ghadaq*, a hapax), 71:11–12 (*midrār*, streams); 14:7's *la-azīdannakum* with **you** as object; and 34:13's "work in gratitude." **[INFERRED]:** that the choice of *li-rabbika* foregrounds an *ongoing nurturing* relationship (well-grounded in the proven "rear" sense); that 14:7's increase is an *enlargement of the self/capacity* rather than a transactional payout (the object *-kum* is textual; the "growth not goods" reading is a fair inference, ~80%); and that *shukr* is *enacted* gratitude (explicit at 34:13). The unifying picture — a Lord who keeps giving, met by a gratitude that grows the giver — rests throughout on the continuity the surah's own word *rabb* already carries.
