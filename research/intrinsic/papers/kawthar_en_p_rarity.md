## 5.27 The rarity stratum: singularity, distinctiveness, and the rare-root skeleton

The validation of §5.26 located al-Kawthar's distinctiveness *negatively* — it is ordinary in how its words bind the corpus web. This section locates it *positively*, by descending to the rarity floor (roots used once) and climbing back up the rarity spectrum. Every claim here is corpus-internal (rasm root counts) and null-anchored; the analogies that motivated the probes — percolation, contagion, neutral evolution — are admitted only as suppliers of *null models*, never as metaphors.

### 5.27.1 The frequency spectrum is near-neutral — so hapax abundance is not, by itself, remarkable

Of 1,702 distinct roots over 51,044 root-tokens, **408 (24%) occur exactly once** (hapax). Fisher's neutral log-series — the standard null for a lexicon's frequency spectrum — predicts **337** singletons for these N and S; the observed 408 gives an **obs/pred ratio of 1.21** (slope of φₖ ≈ −1.26, Zipfian) [MEASURED] ({{F14}}). The reading is deflationary and important: roughly four-fifths of the hapax count is the *generic* baseline any lexicon of this size produces, so "the Qurʾān has many once-only words" is **not** a special fact. There is a *modest* heavy-tail excess (≈ +71 roots, 1.21×) whose source — Arabic root-morphology versus genuinely singular subject-matter — we leave open [MEASURED for the ratio; the cause is INFERRED/untested]. The consequence for this paper is precise: al-Kawthar's distinction cannot be the *existence* of hapax; it must be *where* they pool.

### 5.27.2 Singularity is clustered, and al-Kawthar is a validated outlier

It pools. Hapax are over-dispersed across verses (index of dispersion 1.20; 32 verses carry ≥2 hapax versus ~13 expected by chance) and concentrate by sūra (Gini 0.57) [MEASURED]. Two pre-registered, null-tested results place al-Kawthar at the extreme:

- **Density.** At 0.67 hapax/verse — ≈ 10× the corpus mean — al-Kawthar sits at the **97th percentile** of a random-verse null (and a contiguous-window null) [MEASURED]. (It is 2nd of the short sūras after al-Quraysh; dense singularity is partly a short-sūra class trait, so the corpus-level outlier status is the claim, not uniqueness.)
- **Distinctiveness.** Defining a hapax as *distinct* when its rare (non-ubiquitous) context is shared with no other once-only root, **174 of 408 hapax (≈ 43%, range 25–50% across thresholds) are distinct**. Both of al-Kawthar's hapax — *naḥr* and *abtar* — are distinct, and a window holding **≥2 distinct hapax occurs in only 0.37% of random 3-verse draws**: al-Kawthar is in the **top ~0.4%** [MEASURED]. *Naḥr* and *abtar* therefore **stand out** in a precise, positive sense: their lexical environments are unrepeated anywhere in the Book.

So the surah's distinctiveness is **lexical singularity, twice validated** (density and distinctiveness), in clean contrast to its *ordinary* web-binding (§5.26).

### 5.27.3 The rarity ladder: a specificity gradient

Stratifying roots by **spread** (number of sūras a root touches), the spectrum decays smoothly — 476 roots in one sūra, 229 in exactly two, 139 in three, 93 in four … — and mean idf falls monotonically with the stratum (8.66 at spread 1 → 5.34 at spread 20) [MEASURED]. The semantic character tracks this gradient: the rarest tier is *concrete, once-named objects* (بصل onion, عدس lentils, قثء cucumber — the foods of 2:61), giving way through concrete events (رعد thunder, صبغ dye) to abstractions at higher spread (جوع hunger, حرص greed) [the gradient MEASURED; the concrete→abstract characterization INFERRED from samples]. al-Kawthar's own seven roots form a cross-section of the whole ladder — *ربب* at the 100th percentile (near-universal), *kathra* and *ṣalāt* common-connective, *ʿaṭā* rare-connective, *shaniʾ* at spread 2, and *naḥr*/*abtar* on the spread-1 floor — ({{F15}}; {{T13}}) — which is exactly why the surah reads at once as singular (its floor) and ordinary in the web (its connective roots dominate the binding score of §5.26) [MEASURED placements; INFERRED synthesis].

### 5.27.4 The rare-root skeleton, and roles in the flow of meaning

Roots of spread 2 form a **rare-bridge graph**: 229 such roots weld 209 unique sūra-pairs, with the long legal sūras (2–7) most densely bridged and sūras 2 and 18 the highest-degree hubs [MEASURED]. al-Kawthar's single rare bridge is **شنء → al-Māʾida (5)** — the one statistically notable lexical tie it has (p ≈ 0.0005, §5.26). On a directed *dependency* graph (edge a→b when b reliably accompanies a, P(b|a) ≥ 0.25) — the internal, corpus-only analog of propagation — al-Kawthar's roots take sharply different roles: *shaniʾ* is a far-reaching **source** (downstream reach 155), *rabb* a terminal **sink** (in-degree 224), and *naḥr*/*abtar* are **off-graph islands** (too rare to participate) [MEASURED]. *Honesty bounds:* the rare-bridge graph "percolates" to 83% of sūras at spread 2, but 209 edges over 114 nodes percolate under generic random-graph theory too, so percolation here is **not** a design signal; likewise the dependency sinks (قول، ءله، کون) are dominated by ubiquitous roots and are largely generic. What is *non-generic* is the **role-differentiation** of al-Kawthar's roots across that flow — source, sink, island — and the discrete *shaniʾ* twin.

### 5.27.5 Burstiness rises with frequency — and is *not* a rarity feature

The contagion lens yields one rigorous, valence-blind statistic — burstiness B of a root's recurrences along the canonical sequence (B>0 = clustered/self-exciting; 0 = Poisson) — but it must be read **stratified by frequency**, or the ubiquitous end confounds it ({{F16}}). It does: burstiness rises monotonically with frequency. **Rare roots (5–15 occurrences) are near-Poisson** (median B ≈ −0.04), with only weak above-null clustering (41% bursty vs 18%); only **common and ubiquitous roots cluster strongly** (B +0.16 to +0.23; 96–100% bursty) [MEASURED]. So "the lexicon is self-exciting" is a **common-word property** — driven by topical recurrence of frequent vocabulary — **not a feature of the rare stratum.** For al-Kawthar this sharpens the picture rather than adding to it: its rare roots do not recur at all (hapax) and even the rare band barely clusters, so the surah's singularity is *clean* rarity, not embedded in a bursty rare regime. The honest residue of the diffusion lens here is therefore small and frequency-confined; we report it as such, not as a corpus-wide "contagion."

### 5.27.6 The spread of the message, made measurable — and found to be *contained*

The diffusion lens earns one substantive, internal result if "spread" is operationalized as a *measurable statistic on the rasm with a null*. We model concept-spread as **independent-cascade (IC) diffusion** on the directed dependency graph (edge a→b with transmission probability P(b | a)): seed a root, let activation cascade stochastically, and measure **reach** — the fraction of the lexicon activated — against a **degree-preserving rewired null** ({{F17}}). The result is striking and the opposite of free contagion: in the real network a seeded concept reaches only **~1–2% of the lexicon (median reach 0.010)**, whereas the rewired null reaches **20–40× more (median ≈ 0.33)** [MEASURED, null-validated]. The Qurʾān's conceptual network is therefore **anti-diffusive relative to random** — cascades stay trapped because the graph is strongly **clustered/modular**; meaning is organised into tight thematic neighbourhoods that *localise* rather than broadcast it (consistent with the corpus's known high modularity). al-Kawthar's roots are low spreaders throughout; *rabb* reaches the *least* (1st pct) despite being the top dependency *sink* — it is where concepts **converge**, not a disseminator — and *naḥr*/*shaniʾ*/*abtar* are islands (reach 0) [MEASURED]. (The super-spreaders, by contrast, are mid-frequency roots locked in dense legal/ritual clusters — *kamal*, *raḍaʿ*, *damm*, *khinzīr* — not the theological core.) This is the admissible form of "spread of the message": the *internal* propagation of meaning, which the text **contains by design of its modular structure**. The *social/historical* spread of the message among people — genuine diffusion — remains external to the corpus and outside this program's evidence [the containment result MEASURED; the "by design" reading INFERRED].

**Synthesis.** Read from the rare end, al-Kawthar is sharply characterised: it concentrates **maximally singular and distinct vocabulary** (validated, §5.27.2) at the **islands of a rarity spectrum that is otherwise near-neutral** (§5.27.1), threading the whole specificity ladder (§5.27.3) with a single statistically-real connective tie (*shaniʾ* → al-Māʾida) and otherwise sitting off the corpus's rare skeleton (§5.27.4); and the corpus that holds it **contains** the spread of meaning in modular neighbourhoods rather than broadcasting it (§5.27.6). Singular, distinct, and — in the web — peripheral; each half now null-tested.
