# ميسر للذکر (54:17) — "made easy to remember": FINDING (facet 1 of N)

CLAIM (one facet): memorability is supported by METRICAL REGULARITY — verses within a
sura are more uniform in length than chance. (No phonology; pure root-counts.)

STATISTIC: M = verse-count-weighted mean within-sura CV of verse length (lower = more
regular). Suras with >=4 verses.

RESULT (two nulls, pre-registered + the obvious confound-control):
- Weak null (global regroup):        M_obs 0.494 vs 0.738  -> z=-85,  33% more regular.
- Stronger null (within-period):     M_obs 0.494 vs 0.678  -> z=-86,  27% more regular.
- 0/300 nulls reached observed in both.

VERDICT: SUPPORTED but UN-ISOLATABLE. Suras are metrically tighter than chance, and this
survives the revelation-period confound (33% -> 27%, still z~-86). Real structure.
HOWEVER: the internal method CANNOT separate "designed for memory" from "chapters are
coherent units" — every stronger internal null exposes another confound (period ->
nuzul-drift -> ...). Cleanly isolating memorability needs a COMPARATOR (other coherent
texts), which this program declines. So: real metrical regularity, NOT a clean
memorability claim.

CONTRAST WITH MATHANI: mathani's null ATE most of the effect (honest moderate positive);
here the effect is huge but the NULL is the weak link, not the effect. Different failure
modes; both filed honestly. "The null you choose is half the claim" (M01).

SCOPE / LIMITS: one facet (rhythm) of "easy to remember"; descriptive, internal, no
comparator. The more direct facet — end-rhyme / fasila regularity — is phonological
(recited layer, parked) and is the natural next probe when that layer is in scope.
