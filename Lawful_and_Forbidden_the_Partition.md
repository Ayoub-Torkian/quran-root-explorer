# The Lawful and the Forbidden — الحلال والحرام
### A concept cross-referencing study (form ↔ meaning ↔ grammar), read from the whole dataset

**The journey to here.** From the Concept Atlas we began reading concepts by their layers and found that
concepts have *different shapes*: **light / darkness** is an *oriented polarity axis* (a switch with a
direction); **mercy** is a *gradient / field* (unbounded, graded, no clean zero). This third study is chosen to
be a **third type** — a **partition**: a domain carved into disjoint regions by an explicit boundary. Reading
the three together is the point — it shows the atlas holds genuinely different structures, and that "what shape
is this concept?" is itself a finding.

**Sense-resolution first.** Two roots, carefully separated:
- **حلل** (51×) — the *lawful* sense (أحل · یحل · حلالا · حلال) is in view; the *alight / untie* sense (حل
  "to alight", محل "place of sacrifice") is excluded.
- **حرم** (83×) — the *forbidden* and the *sacred* senses are in view (they are, as §3 shows, **one concept**);
  the *deprived* sense (محروم · محرومون, "the destitute") is excluded.

---

## 1. The measured layer — the partition is asymmetric: a small marked exception inside a default ground  [TEXT · MEASURED]

The domain of foods, acts and relations is cut in two by a matched pair of boundary-operators, both passive
(the line is *drawn upon* people, not by them):

- **حُرِّمَ عَلَيْکُم** — "forbidden **upon** you" — **9 verses** (2:173, 4:23, 5:3, 6:119, 6:145, 7:157 …), and
  each opens a **closed, enumerated list** — the carrion/blood/pork list (2:173, 5:3), the forbidden marriages
  (4:23).
- **أُحِلَّ لَکُم** — "made lawful **for** you" — **9 verses** (2:187, 5:1, 5:4, 5:5, 5:87 …), but these are
  **open and general** (*الطیبات* "the good things", game of the sea, etc.).

The keystone is **6:145**: *قُل لَّا أَجِدُ فِي مَا أُوحِيَ إِلَيَّ مُحَرَّمًا … إِلَّا أَن يَکُونَ مَيْتَةً …*
— "I find nothing forbidden … **except** [a short list]." The grammar of **exception (إلا)** states the shape
outright: the **forbidden is the marked, bounded, small set; the lawful is the unmarked default — everything
else.** This is not a symmetric 50/50 split. It is a **small ḥarām carved out of a vast default-ḥalāl.**

---

## 2. Grammar ↔ semantics — the line has exactly one legitimate Author  [TEXT · MEASURED]

A partition needs someone to draw it, and the text is emphatic that **only God may**. Three verses forbid
humans from setting the boundary themselves — and each ties private line-drawing to **lying about God**:

- **16:116** *وَلَا تَقُولُوا لِمَا تَصِفُ أَلْسِنَتُکُمُ الْکَذِبَ هَـذَا حَلَالٌ وَهَـذَا حَرَامٌ لِّتَفْتَرُوا
  عَلَى اللّهِ الْکَذِبَ* — "Do not say, of the lie your tongues describe, 'this is lawful and this is
  forbidden,' inventing a lie against God."
- **10:59** *قُلْ آللّهُ أَذِنَ لَکُمْ أَمْ عَلَى اللّهِ تَفْتَرُونَ* — "Did God permit you, or do you invent
  about God?"
- **66:1** *لِمَ تُحَرِّمُ مَا أَحَلَّ اللّهُ لَک* — "Why do you forbid what God has made lawful?"

So the partition's **integrity is bound to truthfulness about God**: to move the line is to fabricate. The
boundary is not a human convention but a single authorised decree — which is exactly why the operators are
passive (*ḥurrima / uḥilla*, "was forbidden / was made lawful").

---

## 3. Form ↔ meaning — the word for "forbidden" is the word for "sacred": ḥarām = *the bounded*  [TEXT root-link · INFERRED]

The deepest cross-reference is again in the **root**. **حرم** gives both **"forbidden"** (ḥarām food, drink,
act) **and "sacred / inviolable"**:
- the **Sacred Mosque** *الْمَسْجِد الْحَرَام* — **15×**;
- the **sacred months** *الأَشْهُر الْحُرُم / الشَّهْر الْحَرَام* — **5×**;
- the **sacred, secure town** *بَلَدًا آمِنًا / حَرَمًا آمِنًا* — **3×**; and the *ḥurumāt*, "sacred
  inviolables" (2×).

The forbidden and the sacred are **the same act seen from two sides**: to make a thing ḥarām is to **draw a
boundary around it** — the sacred is fenced-off (may not be *violated*), the forbidden is fenced-off (may not
be *taken*). The concept's own name **means "to set a boundary."** A partition concept, fittingly, is named
after the *line* itself. *(The shared root is **[TEXT]**; that "boundary" unifies forbidden+sacred is
**[INFERRED]**, though the text hands it to us — you enter the Sacred Mosque by *not violating* it, exactly as
you keep the ḥarām food by *not taking* it.)*

---

## 4. The criterion — the line tracks ṭayyib (good) vs khabīth (impure)  [TEXT]

The partition is not arbitrary; it maps onto a quality. **7:157** describes the Prophet as one who
*يُحِلُّ لَهُمُ الطَّيِّبَاتِ وَيُحَرِّمُ عَلَيْهِمُ الْخَبَائِثَ* — "**makes lawful for them the good things
(ṭayyibāt) and forbids them the impure (khabāʾith)**." Accordingly the lawful is repeatedly named *ḥalālan
ṭayyiban*, "lawful **and good**" (2:168, 5:88, 8:69, 16:114; and 5:87 warns *do not forbid the ṭayyibāt God has
made lawful*). So the boundary of §1 rides on a criterion: **lawful ≈ wholesome; forbidden ≈ corrupt.**

---

## 5. Where it plugs into the web

1. **Forbidden ↔ the sacred** (same root) — the fenced-off precinct, months, town; law and sanctity are one
   act of bounding.
2. **Lawful ↔ the good (ṭayyib) ↔ provision (rizq)** — 10:59 frames the whole dispute as being about *rizq*
   ("the provision God sent down, which you made some forbidden and some lawful"): the partition is drawn across
   the field of God's provision.
3. **Moving the line ↔ fabrication against God (iftirāʾ)** — §2; the partition's neighbour on the "wrongdoing"
   side is *lying about God*, which is where private permitting/forbidding is filed.

---

## 6. The concept's structural TYPE — a *bounded partition* (three shapes, side by side)

| | **Light / darkness** | **Mercy** | **Lawful / forbidden** |
|---|---|---|---|
| type | oriented **polarity axis** | **gradient / field** | **bounded partition** |
| boundary | a clean switch, no middle | **none** — "encompasses all things" | **an explicit drawn line** — and the concept is *named* after it (ḥarām) |
| internal shape | binary + one direction | continuous degree, no zero | **default region + a small marked exception** (6:145 "nothing forbidden except…") |
| symmetry | asymmetric poles (1 light / many darknesses) | graded, God at the apex | asymmetric (large default-ḥalāl / small enumerated-ḥarām) |
| who acts | God *brings* one across (dark→light) | one is *within*, can partake | **God alone *draws* the line; humans moving it = fabrication** |
| criterion | guidance vs misguidance | — (unbounded) | ṭayyib (good) vs khabīth (impure) |

Three concepts, three genuinely different geometries — a **switch**, a **field**, and a **fence**. That they do
not share one structure is the mission's point: mapping a concept includes naming *what kind of thing it is*.

## 7. Honesty ledger — measured vs read

- **[TEXT · MEASURED]:** the matched operators (ḥurrima ʿalaykum 9 / uḥilla lakum 9) and the enumerated-vs-default
  asymmetry with its keystone 6:145; the God-only authorship (16:116, 10:59, 66:1); the ṭayyib/khabīth criterion
  (7:157; ḥalāl+ṭayyib 5×); ḥarām = sacred across Mosque (15×), months (5×), town (3×), ḥurumāt (2×).
- **[INFERRED]:** that "boundary/bounding" is the single notion unifying *forbidden* and *sacred*; that the
  partition is "a small exception inside a default ground" as a structural claim (the counts support it; the
  framing is mine).
- **[REPORT] (flagged, not used):** the jurists' five-fold scale (wājib/mandūb/mubāḥ/makrūh/ḥarām) and the
  "clear ḥalāl, clear ḥarām, doubtful between" hadith are later refinements — corroborative, but the *text's own*
  structure is the two-region partition with a default, above.

**Confidence:** the *partition + asymmetry + single-author* structure ~95% — it is counting plus the explicit
exception grammar of 6:145. The *ḥarām-as-boundary reading* ~85% — the shared root and the sacred-precinct usage
make it near-explicit. What would deepen it: test whether a **graded middle** exists in the text itself
(*makrūh*-like "better to avoid" language, e.g. *رِجْسٌ مِّنْ عَمَلِ الشَّيْطَانِ* framings) — i.e. is the
partition truly two-valued, or does it shade at the edge?

## 8. Recommendation

1. **(Recommended) Bank the three as one close-up: "Three shapes of a concept — switch · field · fence."**
   Light/dark, mercy, and ḥalāl/ḥarām side by side, each with its measured signature and a one-glance geometry
   icon. *Why #1: the trio now demonstrates the structural-diversity mandate concretely and gives the atlas a
   reusable "what type is this concept?" lens — worth strictly more assembled than as three separate files.*
2. **Add a *ladder* (already in hand: the heart's ~27 states) and a *frozen pair* (سماء·أرض) to round the
   typology to five named types.** *Why: completes the small catalogue of shapes the mission asks for.*
3. **Test the partition's edge** — does the text carve a graded middle (a makrūh-like zone), or is it strictly
   two-valued? *Why: smallest; directly answers the open question in §7.*
