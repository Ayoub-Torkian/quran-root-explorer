## 1. The core question (reframed by the user — this is the current frame)

Classic statistical measures (entropy, mutual information, repetition, compression) are
**universal across texts and languages**, so they are **mastery-blind** — they cannot tell
a masterpiece from ordinary writing. Confirmed empirically (they fail to separate
Shakespeare from Austen). Therefore the method is **positive-control-first**:

> First find a measure that actually separates a *known master* from ordinary writers in a
> language. Only a validated "mastery detector" earns the right to be applied to the Qur'an.

## 7. METHODOLOGY RULES (locked — do not relax)

- **G10 invariance gate**: a cross-text metric is inadmissible unless its verdict survives
  (a) equal sample size (fixed-N windows for EVERY text), AND (b) ≥2 tokenizations, tested
  against a **same-language ordinary baseline**. Char/rasm scale satisfies (b) by construction.
- **Positive-control gate**: a "mastery" measure must first separate a known master from
  ordinary in its language (validated via degradation ladder) before being applied to the Qur'an.
- **Degradation ladder**: real metrics move monotonically L0(original)→L4(scrambled).
- Report **bootstrap P(master window > ordinary window)** and **sd-gaps with real windows**,
  never single-sample point estimates.
